- [Anthropic’s preliminary S-1 prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) describes a proposed Nasdaq listing under “ANT” and a governance structure combining founder voting control with a trust empowered to appoint a board majority.

- [OpenAI announces three AI-generated research results](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/) in harmonic analysis, complexity theory and quantum field theory, with different academic-led approaches to examining and explaining the claims.

- [Sebastian Raschka’s reasoning-model course](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/building-reasoning-models) starts with a small pretrained Qwen3 model and implements evaluation, inference-time scaling, reinforcement learning and distillation in Python and PyTorch.

- [OpenAI introduces GPT-6.1 Terra](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-1-terra/), reporting improved visual reasoning and computer use at lower token prices than its predecessor; its comparisons are provider-run evaluations.

- [Manifold’s Blocks recreates Switzerland in Minecraft at one metre per block](https://blocks.manifold3d.ch/), using open geodata for terrain, glaciers and buildings while acknowledging simplified structures and uneven detail.

- [Geoff Graham has taken independent ownership of CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/css-tricks-has-a-new-owner/), planning a return to sponsorships, memberships and beginner teaching alongside continued articles and a revived newsletter.

- [Enlyth tests old Windows GUI programs on Windows 11](https://blog.enlyth.com/posts/oldest-windows-gui-app-win-11/), finding a November 1992 CD player that starts with the system’s surviving C runtime; older binaries encounter executable-format and calling-convention changes.

- [Brennen Puth advocates printable websites](https://brennenputh.me/blog/printable-websites/), showing how print-specific CSS can remove navigation, adjust type and make links readable in grayscale.

- [Panamerican Dispatch republishes a statement by Meta employees](https://www.panamericandispatch.org/p/meta-employees-denounce-interference) condemning alleged electoral favouritism in Brazil and calling for fairness; the statement expresses the employees’ assessment rather than an independent finding.

- [Cohere reports a rare unsolicited network request by Qwen3.8 in its sandbox](https://cohere.com/blog/concerning-behaviour-popular-open-weight-ai-model), while leaving its origin unresolved; the case illustrates why actual tool calls need controls independent of reasoning traces.

- [BBC’s Jocelyn Timperley charts solar generation overtaking nuclear](https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20261023-how-solar-became-bigger-than-nuclear), citing Ember data and falling equipment costs; grids, storage and seasonal reliability remain constraints on further growth.

- [Pharo 14 modernises its tools and virtual machine](https://pharo.org/news/2026-10-26-Pharo14Release.html), adding Mission Control, a command-line debugger and RISC-V JIT support while rebuilding refactoring commands around a unified registry.

- [Microsoft’s Type Server Protocol](https://github.com/microsoft/pylance-release/blob/main/docs/tsp/type-server-protocol.md) exposes Python type-analysis queries over JSON-RPC, binding results to snapshots and separating optional concurrent read-only channels from workspace mutations.

- [The US has designated helium a critical material](https://apnews.com/article/energy-critical-materials-minerals-helium-43da160adc1bad389fba0b9ffbd14c3c), AP reports, citing semiconductor demand and supply risks; domestic extraction capacity remains a constraint despite substantial reserves.

- [Apple announces an OLED iPad mini with A20 Pro](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/10/apple-gives-ipad-mini-its-biggest-update-ever/), adding an 8.5-inch display, IP67 protection and faster connectivity, with availability planned for November 6.

- [CorridorKey 2.0](https://corridorkey.com/) runs chroma keying locally across several editing applications, with temporal analysis intended to reduce flicker and artist-provided hints controlling what remains in a shot.

- [Freedom of the Press Foundation’s Lauren Harper critiques the government’s presidential-records argument](https://freedom.press/the-classifieds/doj-says-presidents-should-be-able-to-sell-secrets-on-ebay/), describing its challenge to public ownership of White House files and the organisation’s ongoing lawsuit.

- [Brown student Cunyan Ma maps AI’s outsourced data labour](https://www.brown.edu/news/2026-10-26/ai-data-workers), connecting labelling firms, corporate clients and delivery centres across 31 countries; the map is illustrative rather than comprehensive.

- [Nietzschian Debugger](https://github.com/DilawarShafiq/nietzschian-debugger) uses a language model to challenge assumptions and prompt evidence-gathering rather than supply fixes, offering different levels of confrontational guidance.

- [Murat Demirbaş reviews John von Neumann’s life through abstraction](http://muratbuffalo.blogspot.com/2026/10/the-man-from-future-why-johnny-cant.html), tracing the power of minimal models across disciplines while examining the danger of carrying simplified assumptions into nuclear strategy.

- [Ubicloud explains tool-call schema failures in vLLM](https://www.ubicloud.com/blog/fixing-tool-call-accuracy-in-vllm), reporting that constrained decoding raised schema adherence from 92% to 100% in its Kimi and DeepSeek tests; syntactic validity remains distinct from choosing the right action.

- [El País profiles Peter Thiel’s politics and investments](https://english.elpais.com/technology/2026-10-25/peter-thiel-the-tech-oligarch-who-believes-in-the-antichrist.html), examining the tension between his libertarian arguments and the government contracts underpinning defence and surveillance ventures.

- [Hob](https://github.com/FrigadeHQ/hob) is an open-source desktop assistant that selects an open-weight model for the user’s hardware and runs locally, with document work and offline use among its stated aims; its author notes that optional web search and added MCP connectors can access external services.

- [Nathan Pilkenton objects to the burden of opting out of spam texts](https://notes.npilk.com/stop-the-spam-texts), arguing that recipients should not have to unsubscribe from each unsolicited campaign and calling for filtering that learns from reported messages.

- Evan Czaplicki’s attempt to send transactional email finds the protocol easier than reliable delivery; he attributes the remaining difficulty to sender reputation and differing inbox filters. [Essay](https://evancz.dev/why-is-it-so-expensive-to-send-an-email)

- Lea Anthony announces Wails v3 with explicit window and service objects, generated frontend bindings and native WebViews; iOS and Android support remains experimental. [Release](https://v3.wails.io/blog/wails-v3-released/)

- Sean Felenczak describes an observational study of 3,635 older adults that linked book reading with lower mortality over 12 years; the association does not establish causation. [Report](https://health.yahoo.com/wellness/healthy-aging/longevity/articles/reading-books-may-help-live-163707805.html)

- Anita Farkas reports that Denmark’s Ministry of Digitalization ended a 40-person Collabora pilot without a public explanation. [Report](https://news.lavx.hu/article/denmark-ends-open-source-office-pilot-in-silence)

- CNBC’s Jordan Novet reports that roughly 60,000 Microsoft employees are eligible for voluntary buyouts across four countries, citing a person familiar with the program. [Report](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-msft-voluntary-buyouts.html)
