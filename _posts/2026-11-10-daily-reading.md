- [What malloc is doing](https://benmandrew.com/articles/what-is-malloc-doing-anyway) builds an allocator step by step to explain chunks, free lists and demand paging, showing how glibc retains memory to avoid expensive kernel calls and page faults.
- [Claude Fable and Mythos 5.2](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2): Anthropic reports stronger coding and technical-research performance at lower prices, with a shared underlying model and different safeguards; its formalisation and engineering demonstrations remain company-reported results.
- [Foldable Flight](https://www.foldableflight.com/) catalogues paper-airplane designs by difficulty, paper size and flight characteristics, from simple gliders to elaborate jets.
- [A claimed change in Opus reasoning](https://twitter.com/shanemelaugh/status/2119192075505893695): Shane Melaugh says an AI analysis of 1,400 Claude Code sessions found a shift on November 3, an individual report rather than confirmation of a model change.
- [USPS ZIP Code boundaries](https://postalpro.usps.com/zcb) offers polygons derived from delivery addresses and locations, with quarterly updates and separate internal, enterprise and publication terms.
- [Canada’s Real-Time Rail](https://www.payments.ca/canada-launches-real-time-rail-bringing-247-payment-infrastructure-national-economy) launches with round-the-clock settlement, ISO 20022 messages and mandatory central fraud services; participating institutions will expand beyond its initial cohort.
- [Gemini’s connected-file privacy](https://github.com/thisisme6598/gemini_files/): A repository author argues that revised Google disclosures allow personal Drive and Gmail material into human-review pipelines, and reports varying amounts of retrieval during ordinary conversations.
- [RIP, Harvest](https://aphyr.com/posts/427-rip-harvest): Kyle Kingsbury describes a tenfold subscription increase and an alternative plan’s added transaction fee, questioning the cost of a service he valued for simple invoicing.
- [“I saw your HN comment”](https://joshstrange.com/2026/11/09/i-saw-your-hn-comment/): Josh Strange describes personalised sales pitches disguised as genuine questions, and the distrust they create around once-welcome conversations.
- [Fable 5.2 on Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/claude-fable-5-2) leads its Intelligence Index at 65 in the tested Max configuration, while taking substantially more time and money per task than comparable models.
- [Raspberry Pi’s latest price rise](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/11/03/when-will-this-stop-the-raspberry-pi-5-16gb-sbc-now-sells-for-385/): Jean-Luc Aufranc reports the 16 GB Pi 5 at $385, up $80 from April, alongside higher prices for 16 GB Compute Module 5 variants.
- [E-means clustering](https://e-means.github.io/): Swann Bessa and colleagues combine moving-average minibatch updates, automatic parameter selection and dead-cluster revival, reporting lower loss in roughly a tenth of Lloyd’s epochs across their tests.
- [Muse Ember](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember): Meta introduces a low-cost multimodal model for repeated agent tasks, reporting competitive benchmark efficiency; longer prompts carry higher prices and some comparisons use different harnesses.
- [Watched Earth](https://www.watched.earth/) matches predicted satellite passes to public imagery, distinguishing confirmed photographs from possible coverage; its documentation notes that the broad headline count includes passes without published images.
- [Google’s new TPU systems](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/tpu-8i-max-and-8tx/) specialise existing silicon for confidential inference and larger memory bandwidth, pairing hardware configurations with software for tiered model state and idle-agent suspension.
- [SSH-agent forwarding in Codex](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50422628): A submission flags remote access to local authentication; its linked implementation discussion shows the app server configured with a forwarded SSH-agent socket.
- [Next-Concept Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14162): Hermann Kumbong and colleagues propose generating hierarchical text codes in parallel from coarse to fine, reporting large speedups against diffusion baselines on their tested tasks.
- [A nephew’s dream game](https://notesbylex.com/fable-5-2-one-shotted-my-nephews-dream-game): Lex Toumbourou describes an overnight AI-assisted rebuild followed by family playtesting and revisions, growing into a twelve-level game with eighteen characters.
- [What an H100 rental costs](https://github.com/datareceipts/data-receipts/tree/main/109-gpu-pricing) compares identical GPU variants across providers, finding a wide on-demand price spread while distinguishing networking, service guarantees, availability and discounted contracts.
- [Children of Men at twenty](https://reactormag.com/children-of-men-the-future-is-a-mirror-for-the-present/): Kali Wallace connects its restrained worldbuilding and long apparent takes to a political vision that offers hope through protection of others without promising a happy ending.
- [CUI](https://yappologistic.github.io/CUI/) presents a cross-platform C99 interface library with immediate-mode components, keyboard controls and a small native application footprint, drawing its own widgets rather than embedding a browser.
- [DeepX’s second-generation chip](https://www.eetimes.com/deepx-tapes-out-second-gen-chip-projects-6-7x-performance-gain-with-lpddr5x-pim/): Sally Ward-Foxton reports the company’s low-power inference targets and simulated processing-in-memory gains; DX-M2 samples are expected in March 2027, so these are projections rather than shipping performance.
- [A critique of GPUI](https://blog.zortax.de/posts/a-critique-of-gpui) argues for finer-grained tracked reads in complex interfaces, while acknowledging signal systems’ creation and memory costs and the author’s stake in a competing framework.
- [Flide](https://github.com/OmniJev/Flide) uses exact state forking to accelerate repeated decisions over shared context, with workload-dependent speedups reported against other inference engines on an H200.
- Michael Larabel reports that Martin Willi’s queued Linux ChaCha AVX-512 patch processes 16 blocks at once, with tests showing up to 99% faster performance on AMD Zen 5. [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/ChaCha-AVX-512-Linux-7.5)
- BBC reports that M John Harrison won the 2026 Booker Prize for *The End of Everything*, becoming its oldest winner at 81. [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c615j883l8vqt)
- Reviewing Saul Justin Newman’s *Morbid*, Dhruv Khullar examines how unreliable age records can distort longevity research and the claims made for “blue zones.” [The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/07/06/morbid-saul-justin-newman-book-review-eat-your-ice-cream-ezekiel-j-emanuel)
- Aaron Gordon and Polly Mosendz trace a Massachusetts betting cohort over three years, finding that frequent depositors account for a disproportionate share of estimated sportsbook revenue; the industry disputes deposit frequency as a measure of problem gambling. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-sports-betting-debt-bankruptcy/)
- Mark Russinovich’s VENN research preview makes typed decisions over reusable embeddings with small local heads; its documentation warns that off-topic inputs can still receive high-confidence answers. [VENN](https://markrussinovich.github.io/venn-vdm/).
- The ASP.NET Core agent report linked by David Fowler lists 27 merged performance PRs and 62 still open, stresses maintainer approval, and flags measurements that need repeating after review changes. [Experiment update](https://github.com/dotnet/aspnetcore/issues/70219#issuecomment-6587329600).
- Zhang, Peng, Najt, Scholl and Martin report reproducing a Lean formalization of Fermat’s Last Theorem with 1,000 agents, separate sandboxes, Git and shared SQL tables for claiming tasks and recording results. [Multi-agent experiment](https://claude.dev/blog/safely-scaling-multi-agent-teams-with-fable-5-2/).
