- [Apollo's Torsten Slok](https://www.apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/no-signs-of-ai-payoff-outside-the-tech-sector) argues that flat revenue per worker and margins outside the largest technology firms show little aggregate financial payoff from AI so far.

- [Steve Blank](https://steveblank.com/2026/10/24/europe-gets-a-wake-up-call/) argues that dependence on American technology creates sovereignty risks for Europe and an opening for alternative suppliers.

- [Goose](https://aardappel.github.io/goose/) explores memory-safe systems programming with compiler-managed data stacks and inline layouts, trading away some dynamic language features to simplify allocation and lifetimes.

- [Joseph Politano](https://www.apricitas.io/p/ai-and-the-fall-of-the-creative-class) examines declining US creative-industry employment while stressing how difficult it is to separate AI's effects from consolidation, offshoring and shifts in media consumption.

- [Aikido](https://www.aikido.dev/blog/python-packages-vulnerable-node2shell-brotli-cve-2026-111497) traces the Brotli decoder flaw across runtimes and HTTP libraries, noting that bundled copies require their own updates.

- [FreeDNS](https://freedns.afraid.org/) offers DNS hosting and dynamic updates, including shared-domain subdomains and IPv6 support.

- [Google’s Finnish data-centre clearing](https://nypost.com/2026/10/24/business/google-illegally-tore-down-250k-trees-for-finland-data-center-infuriating-locals-report/) drew regulatory intervention, the New York Post reports; Google said it believed it had approval and pledged to replant trees.

- [GPU Unlock](https://github.com/Zagitta/gpu-unlock) reports higher tensor throughput on several Ampere GPUs through a Linux kernel module; its tests show larger gains in compute-bound work than memory-bound decoding.

- [GVS5H](https://github.com/slee-persis/GVS5H) uses a shared file ledger to coordinate coding agents without further training, with reported gains on its LiveCodeBench test set.

- [Meta restricted Lula’s accounts](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/10/16/meta-employees-say-lulas-accounts-restricted-in-brazil-election-report), according to employee messages reported by AP; Meta attributed the restrictions to technical issues and denied favouring a candidate.

- [Cerebras explains its model pipeline](https://www.cerebras.ai/blog/how-we-deploy-trillion-parameter-frontier-models-on-cerebras): weights stay in on-chip memory while activations move between wafers, with cache capacity limiting concurrent requests.

- [SpeedCrunch](https://www.speedcrunch.org/) is a scientific calculator with up to 50 digits of precision, custom functions and units, and vector and matrix support.

- [PHP Foundation director Elizabeth Barron tells Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/php-foundation-under-funded-with-one-full-time-staff-member-director-says-2026-10-23/) that proposed code changes have nearly doubled while reviewer numbers have not; the foundation has one full-time employee and 14 mostly part-time contractors.

- [EPFL’s LogSync chip](https://actu.epfl.ch/news/neural-chip-decodes-interactions-between-brain-r-2/) extracts signals across brain regions at under 200 microwatts; validation used existing human recordings and stimulation experiments in rats.

- [TorrentFreak found untraceable citations](https://torrentfreak.com/ai-assisted-paper-on-z-library-cites-a-torrentfreak-article-that-doesnt-exist/) in an AI-assisted Z-Library paper, including an article attributed to TorrentFreak itself; Taylor & Francis says it is investigating.

- [Microsoft reportedly declined Netflix’s proposed Xbox rights deal](https://www.darkhorizons.com/xbox-turns-down-netflix-500-million-offer/), with Dark Horizons citing Puck’s account of a $500 million offer and an $800 million counteroffer.

- [Dan Luu argues for more ambitious software projects](https://danluu.com/ambitious/), describing bespoke tools and experiments made economical by coding agents while stressing the continuing value of engineering judgement and verification.

- [Dream Labs introduces Dream-1](https://dreamlabs.ai/research/dream-1/), a robot policy designed to transfer across bimanual robots through shared gripper poses; its research beta still depends on inverse kinematics and struggles with unfamiliar bodies and tasks.

- [Dave Gauer explores small programs and languages](https://ratfactor.com/cards/pl-small) as approachable models whose bounded complexity makes experimentation and understanding easier.

- [Wayne’s case for Gopher in 2026](https://gopher.muffinlabs.com/sdf.org/users/nervtoji/phlog/001.txt) values a quieter place to write and learn how the tools work, while acknowledging the conveniences modern computing brings.

- [Dave Gauer argues that limits invite play](https://ratfactor.com/cards/limitations), drawing on children’s experiments with a simple browser feature and the creative discipline imposed by small budgets.

- The Broccoli2Shell overview traces a Brotli decoder flaw through automatic HTTP decompression and bundled library copies, explaining why updating a system package may leave application runtimes exposed. [Overview](https://broccoli2shell.com/)

- Christian Brauner’s merged systemd change enables Rust for new leaf components, using Meson and existing C interfaces while leaving existing C components in place. [Pull request](https://github.com/systemd/systemd/pull/44324)

- In James Tapper’s interview, Terence Tao argues that AI-generated proofs need explanation and refinement, and calls for a more collaborative mathematical culture; OpenAI says it is improving how it shares results. [The Observer](https://observer.co.uk/news/science-technology/article/terence-tao-ai-turns-beautiful-mathematics-into-toxic-waste)

- [COBOL on Wheelchair is a small GNU COBOL web framework designed to run through Apache CGI.](https://github.com/azac/cobol-on-wheelchair)

- [Steven Strogatz describes mathematics’ tension between obtaining results, understanding proofs and sharing the experience of discovery, through contrasting reactions from Cornell colleagues.](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/2026/10/ai-math-human-future/689133/)
