- [Standardizing Japanese car parts](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a74490092/toyota-nissan-honda-standardize-parts-lower-costs/): Jack Fitzgerald reports that Toyota, Nissan and Honda plan common steel criteria, plastics protocols and wiring colors to reduce costs while preserving visible brand identities.
- [Anthropic’s projected power mix](https://betterdatacenterproject.com/2026/11/16/how-ai-is-powered-a-breakdown-of-byop-and-grid-power/): Better Data Center Project estimates that gas and coal would supply almost all identified capacity in 2028, using marginal grid generation and assumptions about site delivery and utilization.
- [Perplexity and intelligence](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/): Jakub Pachocki proposes shared development limits tied to prediction loss and monitoring milestones, while acknowledging dataset differences and gains from reasoning that pretraining loss does not capture.
- [Silicon Valley’s changing incentives](https://www.oreilly.com/radar/what-happened-to-silicon-valley/): Tim O’Reilly argues that shareholder incentives push successful platforms from creating value toward extracting it, and calls for open interfaces, portability and business models that sustain customers’ livelihoods.
- [Birdwatching in GTA 6](https://www.audubon.org/magazine/birds-of-gta-6-field-guide-vice-city-wildlife): Nicholas Lund describes finding the game’s 60 listed bird species, praising recognizable South Florida habitats while noting unrealistic species abundance and geography.
- [OpenRouter’s provider review](https://openrouter.ai/blog/announcements/data-processing-incidents-on-inference-providers/): OpenRouter says it off-boarded three providers and removed 125 endpoints from zero-retention eligibility, including precautionary removals, and is introducing ongoing technical checks.
- [Worker 755](https://github.com/gulivan/worker755) reverses the usual agent workflow: a human follows an LLM manager’s timed instructions in a simulated filesystem, while hidden checks track mistaken approvals and the manager writes a performance review.
- [A local AI setup](https://www.gpupartner.com/blog/beginners-guide-local-ai-inkling-qwen-unsloth): Jerry James walks through Unsloth Desktop on Windows, explaining the trade between model precision, available memory and context length, with password-protected sharing restricted to the home network.
- [Open Social Web Alliance](https://osw-alliance.net/en/) proposes public funding and shared governance for interoperable social networks, identifying infrastructure, funding, governance, appeal and a public mandate as barriers to broader adoption.
- [Fast Anything](https://github.com/EL4CTEO/fast-anything) profiles deterministic hotspots for selective Rust ports, checking recorded calls and derived edge cases before enabling them; the checks establish agreement on tested inputs rather than general equivalence.
- [Consciousness and computation](https://www.noemamag.com/the-mythology-of-conscious-ai/): Anil Seth argues that living embodiment may be necessary for consciousness, acknowledges that this remains a minority view, and distinguishes the risks of conscious machines from those of machines that merely seem conscious.
- [Masullo formalization progress](https://claude.ai/artifact/CtQyX8qfYAF822bgHJHHE3): A project dashboard separates completed Lean proofs from proofs resting on open lemmas, and records false or incomplete manuscript statements alongside outstanding repairs.
- [Backblaze’s privacy-policy change](https://www.neowin.net/news/backblaze-customers-worried-of-personal-data-salesharing-after-policy-update/): Sayan Sen reports concern about advertising-related sharing of customer information, distinguishing that information from backed-up files and noting that the cited file-privacy FAQ dates to 2020.
- [Joining the NTP Pool](https://www.jeffgeerling.com/blog/2026/i-joined-the-ntp-pool/): Jeff Geerling places a public Stratum 2 VPS between the pool and his private GNSS-backed clock, explaining the exposure, persistent traffic and uptime commitment that public time service brings.
- [Canada’s proposed news funding](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/11/a-government-that-gets-it-canada-recommits-to-federal-funding-for-news/): Sarah Scire reports a budget proposal to make local-reporting support permanent and extend newsroom salary credits, while describing eligibility gaps and recruitment problems in underserved communities.
- [P versus NP, still open](https://pequalnp.com/): An agent-fleet project distinguishes checked lemmas from conjectures, refutations and abandoned approaches, explicitly warning that its counts are neither probabilities nor a solution to the problem.
- [Nespresso’s Project Olympus](https://alvinbunk.wordpress.com/2026/11/01/nespresso-project-olympus/): Alvin Bunk publishes what he describes as internal instructions to reduce coffee per capsule, contrasting the document’s claimed unchanged taste and caffeine with customer concerns; the document’s provenance is his account.
- [AI’s climate costs](https://www.bloodinthemachine.com/p/ai-is-becoming-a-disaster-for-the): Brian Merchant argues that data-center emissions estimates miss important sources and calls for clearer disclosure of projected pollution and health effects before new facilities are approved.
- [Nyx](https://nyx.net/) describes a donation-supported, volunteer-run nonprofit offering free email, web hosting, Usenet and Linux shell accounts, preserving a model of public internet access outside subscription platforms.
- [Canary tokens and AI providers](https://www.dragos.com/blog/ai-supply-chain-security-canary-tokens): HD Moore reports that seeded prompts exposed breaches, reseller access and retention failures; the investigation combines token activity with route tracing because a token alone cannot locate the leak within the chain.
- [GL.iNet’s security update](https://www.gl-inet.com/en-us/blogs/blog/glinet-security-notice-2026-11-20): GL.iNet says attackers installed malware through its cloud management service; blocking that route did not clean already infected devices, so the company recommends model-specific security firmware even when quick checks find no indicator.
- [The discrete Fourier transform](https://eli.thegreenplace.net/2026/notes-on-the-discrete-fourier-transform-dft/): Eli Bendersky connects finite signal samples, Fourier matrices and reconstruction, showing why spectral leakage can distort frequency interpretation even when the original samples reconstruct correctly.
- [Gender-mirrored conflict prompts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14068): Guangzong Si and colleagues report harsher framing toward male actors across ten tested models, using 1,298 synthetic scenarios; their preprint limits the finding to controlled binary-gender comparisons and notes uncertainty from model-based scoring.
- [The Riemann Handbook](https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-handbook) translates a Lean development into mathematical prose, while explicitly warning that neither its complete build nor the written statements have been independently checked.
- [Spelunkr](https://spelunkr.tv/) arranges Internet Archive videos into synchronized television channels, offering a free, account-free viewing interface without advertising.
- [Writing for readers](https://danielsada.tech/blog/assume-people-are-not-interested-in-your-blog-post/): Daniel Sada recommends naming the intended audience immediately and putting brief explanations before optional detail, so readers can quickly decide whether to continue.
- Raph Levien urges Rust GUI developers to separate design from execution through clear module interfaces, build on shared text and accessibility infrastructure, and improve compositor integration. [Linebender](https://linebender.org/blog/next-100-guis/)
- Dalya Alberge reports the British Library's proposal to replace three reading rooms with commercial workspaces, setting its funding argument against the staff union's objections.
