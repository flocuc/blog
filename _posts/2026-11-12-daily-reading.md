- [A datacenter’s false smoke alarm](https://michael.bacarella.com/2026/11/10/false-smoke-alarm-jane-street-datacenter/): Michael Bacarella recalls cooling shutting down while sprinkler pipes filled; the near miss led to fleet-wide temperature monitoring that could detect a shared failure hidden by isolated hot-server alerts.
- [An AI-assisted Maya rediscovery](https://pietromiotti.com/latest/maya-site): Pietro Miotti’s agents identified structures in public lidar, but missed a 2025 archaeological paper that had already mapped the site; he corrected the original discovery claim.
- [Remembering Tim Peters](https://discuss.python.org/t/in-memoriam-tim-peters-1952-2026/110028): Guido van Rossum recalls Python’s early co-designer, Timsort’s creator and a patient mentor whose explanations shaped the language’s development culture.
- [AWS Emulator](https://github.com/aws/aws-emulator) offers local API testing without an AWS account; AWS describes agent-written implementations checked against real-service fidelity tests, with engineers designing specifications and merge gates rather than reviewing every change.
- [United’s AI booking restrictions](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-booking-flights/): Megan Cerullo reports the airline’s credential-security and regulatory concerns, alongside analyst Henry Harteveldt’s view that controlling intermediaries and search costs also matters.
- [Secure Enclave SSH keys](https://gatezh.com/posts/macos-secure-enclave-ssh-keys/): Serge Gatezh explains hardware-backed signing with biometric approval, its awkward native prompts and the need for a separate recovery key when a Mac fails.
- [TV makers’ privacy answers](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1012372/tv-privacy-acr-questions): John Higgins compares 17 questions across manufacturers, exposing gaps in responses and differences between optional viewing recognition, essential telemetry and data retained while offline.
- [Sand in WebGL2](https://av.github.io/safe-sand/) presents an interactive browser simulation with different grain types and controls for pouring, pushing and tilting.
- [Gemini Decisions API](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/): Google describes scoring bounded multimodal choices in one pass, returning probabilities for yes/no checks, candidate options and ordered rubrics rather than generating answer text.
- [Unlocking a mining GPU](https://blog.kate.land/posts/using-voltage-glitching-to-unlock-the-a100/): Kate reports recovering disabled memory and compute on CMP 170HX cards through fault injection, with results constrained by each chip’s working hardware.
- [A 6809 game-programming trick](https://blog.moertel.com/posts/2013-12-14-great-old-timey-game-programming-hack.html): Tom Moertel recalls using stack instructions and prearranged graphics data to accelerate drawing, then changing tile order so interrupt damage was repaired before display.
- [Apple’s support forum closure](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1031507/apple-community-support-forum-shutting-down): Jess Weatherbed reports that the community is read-only ahead of its November 20 shutdown; Apple has not specified what will happen to the historical posts.
- [Linear programming for weight edits](https://nathan.rs/posts/weight-space-lp/): Nathan Barry reports matching an agent’s loop-reduction edit on one small Qwen model with eight times fewer changed weights; a nearly constant steering vector recovered much of the effect, limiting broader conclusions about weight-space programming.
- [Work, wages and ownership](https://wilsoniumite.com/2026/11/11/why-are-we-still-working-this-hard/): Wilson argues that productivity gains need not become leisure when wages remain households’ main claim on output, using a simplified economic model to make the case for broader ownership.
- [Atuin Threads](https://blog.atuin.sh/threads-agent-sessions/): Ellie Huxtable introduces searchable agent-session history that can resume across tools and machines, with opt-in capture, local storage and end-to-end encrypted synchronization.
- [Humility before intervention](https://www.experimental-history.com/p/we-need-more-goat-sacrifices): Adam Mastroianni argues for testing social programmes against explicit outcomes and learning from null results, rather than treating good intentions as evidence or failure as a reason to stop investigating.
- [Yogurt](https://github.com/FrigadeHQ/yogurt) is an MIT-licensed meeting-notes application whose creator describes local transcription and summaries, with optional sharing through a local MCP server rather than a required cloud intermediary.
- [Paint-splatter Perl](https://www.mcmillen.dev/sigbovik/): Colin McMillen’s SIGBOVIK satire runs OCR over paint images and finds that 93% yield syntactically valid Perl, illustrating how little valid syntax implies about meaningful computation.
- [Firefox’s fingerprinting defenses](https://blog.mozilla.org/security/2026/11/10/open-research-stronger-privacy-making-firefox-users-harder-to-fingerprint/): Mozilla researchers report reducing identifying differences in screen, processor and rendering measurements, while acknowledging that trackers can adapt and protection varies by browsing mode.
- [Learning faster retrieval](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/faster-retrieval): Apple researchers train small networks on a fixed database and its likely query distribution, improving routing or rewriting queries to trade an upfront training cost for faster approximate search.
- [Emacs in the age of agents](https://commandlinesystems.com/magic.txt) reflects on cheap, personalized reimplementations and the tension between software that follows a user’s wishes and software the user can understand.
- [GitHub Actions lockfiles](https://hugovk.dev/blog/2026/lock-github-actions/): Hugo van Kemenade demonstrates retaining readable version tags while a separate lockfile records immutable commits, repository identities and verification checks.
- [Black Ops II on Apple Silicon](https://github.com/adrbn/bo2-mac) translates a player’s own game ahead of time and reports playable local modes on one M1; the documentation notes interpreter fallbacks, incomplete campaign testing and significant heat.
- [Pensions and AI financing](https://lpeproject.org/blog/your-retirement-savings-are-fueling-the-ai-bubble/): Lenore Palladino argues that pension allocations to private funds expose workers to opaque data-center financing risks and calls for stronger trustee oversight and public investment alternatives.
- Firefox 160 adds on-device speech recognition in 40 languages, accessible MathML in saved PDFs, and Metal rendering for WebGL on macOS. [Release notes](https://www.firefox.com/en-US/firefox/160.0/releasenotes/)
- Hamdi Şahin’s study of an *Iliad* manuscript in Mehmed II’s library suggests a 12th- or 13th-century Istanbul workshop origin and links between different commentary traditions. [Türkiye Today](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/culture/rare-iliad-manuscript-from-ottoman-sultan-mehmed-iis-library-reveals-new-clues-3232134)
- UChicago scholar Dulcey Hunter describes how book challenges constrain teachers and argues that access to difficult literature helps students build empathy. [WTTW](https://news.wttw.com/2026/11/10/uchicago-s-banned-books-scholar-chilling-effect-censorship-efforts-across-us)
- Mudassir Hussain describes Microsoft’s patent application for distributing small AI-training tasks to consenting owners’ idle GPUs in exchange for credits. [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/microsoft-ai-use-your-gpu/)
- eMudhra attributes its weak ECDSA CA keys to a host-side script that truncated private values to one byte; its report distinguishes that defect from a separate RSA key-handling compliance failure and confirms plans to exit public TLS. [Incident report](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2083449#c82)

Levine reports on young plaintiffs seeking to hold xAI liable for abusive images generated from childhood photos; xAI says it prohibits child exploitation and declined comment on pending litigation. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/features/2026-grok-sexualized-childhood-photos/)
- [Epic’s AI MegaJam](https://itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam) runs November 12–15 for Unreal Engine and UEFN games, with teams of up to five people, optional AI tools and a playable build required.

[Kelly Sommers](https://x.com/kellabyte/status/2120702054360334786) stresses another part of that workflow: sequential, readable code makes human review faster.
