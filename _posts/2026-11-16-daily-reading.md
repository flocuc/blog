- [Self-hosting map tiles with Protomaps](https://bruceroettgers.eu/blog/protomaps/): Bruce Röttgers explains how a single PMTiles file and HTTP Range caching can make map hosting inexpensive, with costs tied to storage and actual navigation bandwidth.
- [AI-generated code and open source](https://jakub.jankiewicz.org/blog/ai-code-open-source/): Jakub Jankiewicz argues that code generated with little human input should remain open, citing training data provenance and differing copyright rules.
- [OLED display comfort](https://macanorak.com/the-macbook-pros-oled-pwm-problem-when-a-better-display-becomes-worse-to-use/): Alexander Cohen examines reports of discomfort with the OLED MacBook Pro, distinguishing measured dimming behavior from individual sensitivity and unproven causes.
- [A pool alarm lost in an Outlook outage](https://www1.wdr.de/nrw/rheinland/rhein-erft-kreis/technischer-defekt-chlor-phantasialand-100.html): WDR reports that Phantasialand missed emailed warnings about excess pool chlorine during a Microsoft outage; 15 hotel guests were injured.
- [Warrants for license-plate database searches](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/11/were-asking-appeals-court-require-warrant-alpr-searches): EFF’s Andrew Crocker describes an appeals brief arguing that retrospective vehicle tracking differs from observing a visible plate and should require a warrant.
- [Positional embeddings in LLMs](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-positional-embeddings): Sebastian Raschka walks through absolute embeddings, RoPE and NoPE, explaining how query-key rotations encode relative offsets and how current architectures mix these methods.
- [Things smaller than Turbo Pascal](https://prog21.dadgum.com/116.html): James Hague’s 2011 comparison puts a complete 39,731-byte Turbo Pascal compiler and editor beside larger web assets and utility programs.
- [Modern NES games](https://jordi.inversethought.com/blog/25-of-my-favourite-modern-nes-games/): Jordi surveys 25 independent NES games, from co-op platformers to online fighters, describing how developers extend the old console’s hardware and genres.
- [Emacs as a browser](https://joshblais.com/blog/emacs-as-my-browser/): Joshua Blais describes using EWW for focused reading and documentation, with a conventional browser as a fallback for JavaScript-heavy services.
- [Old Bitly links change hands](https://ransomnews.com/burger-king-tweet-bitly-link-hijack-2026/): Ransomnews documents repurposed short links in old posts; Bitly acknowledged the issue and said it suspended involved accounts and disabled affected links.
- [A Stripe account closure](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50510042): A contractor-lead business owner describes an account closed after a blocked $1,500 saved-card charge, and says appeals did not solicit the customer evidence they offered.
- [Controllers dispute the ground-stop explanation](https://www.commondreams.org/news/duffy-ground-stop-explanation): Brett Wilkins reports controllers’ objections to Sean Duffy’s account of the nationwide outage, alongside the administration’s evolving explanation of a network upgrade failure.
- [Huang’s possible return to Nvidia](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/jensen-huang-discusses-comeback-nvidia-ceo-with-allies-ft-reports-2026-11-15/): Reuters relays an FT report that Jensen Huang discussed returning as chief executive with allies; Nvidia had not responded to Reuters’ request for comment.
- [Handwriting as a font](https://ductus.sorenblank.com/): Ductus provides a letter-by-letter handwriting canvas and font export, with an interface stating that the letters stay on the user’s computer.
- [Books for systems programmers](https://nick-black.com/dankwiki/index.php?title=Book_list_for_streetfighting_computer_scientists): Nick Black’s opinionated reading list ranges from C interfaces and Unix programming to networking, concurrency, algorithms and computational complexity.
- [Vulcan loses an Amazon mission](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/ula-vulcan-centaur-return-to-flight-amazon-leo-internet-satellite-launch): Mike Wall reports that a Vulcan Centaur anomaly ten minutes into flight lost 40 Amazon satellites; ULA had not yet identified the cause.
- [Building an NES emulator in Odin](https://lucypero.com/lucynes.html): Lucy describes how precise CPU–graphics timing and cartridge mappers made the last stages of emulator accuracy much harder than getting initial games running.
- [Clice reaches version 1.0](https://ykiko.me/en/articles/2105384023094834022/): Ykiko introduces a C++ language server with isolated compiler processes, persistent module caches and project tools, and reflects on how coding agents changed its development.
- [Testing congressional stock picks](https://arkanumdata.com/research/house-trades): Arkanum’s study of 8,941 disclosed House trades finds no clear typical stock-picking edge in its 2024–2026 sample; it cautions that aggregate results cannot rule out individual informed trades.
- [Encrypting sensitive NetBSD directories](https://netbsd.org/~nia/disk-encrypt/): Nia’s guide separates lost-device secrecy from tamper resistance, keeping a small bootable root while moving user data, logs and software onto an encrypted filesystem.
- [Kiri container runtime](https://runkiri.com/): Kiri’s beta presents a Docker-compatible runtime that creates containers through one daemon rather than a chain of runtime processes; its published speed comparisons come from its own test machine.
- [Preserving Salon journalism](https://blog.archive.org/2026/11/12/preserving-30-years-of-salon-journalism/): Internet Archive’s Chris Freeland announces an experimental explorer for more than 250,000 archived Salon articles after the publication’s closure.
- [Nvidia after its founder](https://steveblank.com/2026/11/15/whats-next-for-nvidia/): Steve Blank argues that Nvidia’s succession decision must preserve the ability to reinvent its business, rather than rely on executing its existing model.
- Andrew Gallant introduces Jiff 1.0, whose time-zone-aware Rust API distinguishes calendar spans from elapsed time and lets callers choose how to resolve skipped or repeated clock times. [Jiff](https://burntsushi.net/jiff/)
- Cooper Bethea explains how Slack isolated services by availability zone and added gradual traffic draining, giving operators a way to contain ambiguous network failures without first identifying their cause. [Slack Engineering](https://slack.engineering/slacks-migration-to-a-cellular-architecture/)

- Marc Winer reports Domyn’s EUROPA training trials on JUPITER: a roughly 952-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts setup, with completed training, evaluations and release terms still outstanding. [MACCUS](https://maccus.fr/en/eu-open-ai-model-europa/)
