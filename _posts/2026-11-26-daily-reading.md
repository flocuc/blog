- [1Password security incident](https://1password.com/blog/update-on-nov-2026-security-incident): 1Password says an attacker used a compromised employee GitHub account to alter backend services and copy some customer account metadata; vault contents and their encryption keys were unaffected.
- [Aaronson on the latest mathematical claims](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10379): Scott Aaronson describes claimed AI proofs of matrix-multiplication and Fourier-transform bounds as a major change in complexity theory, while cautioning that the specific cryptographic rumors remain unverified.
- [Button Sounds](https://buttons.joshwcomeau.com/): Josh W. Comeau’s collection varies randomly among multiple recordings of each button press and release to avoid the artificial repetition of a single sound sample.
- [Inference-server side channels](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7wzWuLP3HhyBLmKfo/models-can-communicate-via-inference-servers-despite-being): Niranjan Deshpande and David Africa demonstrate model communication through batch-dependent numerical differences and shared KV caches in controlled experiments; separate serving instances, invariant kernels and cache isolation close the demonstrated channels.
- [Cogentic research papers](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/cogentic_research_papers): Google Research lists new papers claiming advances in integer factorization, discrete logarithms, approximate Nash equilibria and graph reconstruction.
- [Life in a retention pond](https://www.vox.com/down-to-earth/511999/home-depot-retention-pond-snorkel-joseph-ricketts): Biologist Joseph Ricketts describes the wildlife beneath a Florida parking-lot pond, while stressing that urban refuges cannot replace intact wetlands.
- [Safari at 120 Hz](https://rida.dev/blog/bypassing-ios-safari-60-fps-limit): Rida F’kih combines scroll-derived input with OffscreenCanvas rendering to report roughly 118 FPS on an iPhone, while warning that the technique depends on WebKit implementation details.
- [GPU Demo Gallery](https://gpu-demo-gallery.pages.dev/): Unofficial browser ports reconstruct 36 graphics demonstrations from 3dfx, NVIDIA and ATI, spanning 1997–2006.
- [Loudness Equalization for PipeWire](https://github.com/xoxorwr/pw-loudness-eq): This project ports Windows’ per-application automatic gain control and output limiter to PipeWire, documenting the reverse-engineered signal processing and unsupported stream types.
- [A shorter GRH formalization](https://github.com/lsernau/grh-lean): Anthropic’s repository offers shorter Lean versions of its claimed Dirichlet L-function proof, explicitly noting that it has passed machine checks but has not been read through by a mathematician.
- [Engineering care and AI tools](https://www.iankduncan.com/engineering/2026-11-19-smart-people-who-give-a-shit-use-tools): Ian K. Duncan argues that AI can expand testing and formal verification when engineers retain ownership, while unverified generated code and management cuts undermine reliability.
- [Dangling Treasury subdomains](https://www.intruder.io/research/taking-over-us-treasury-subdomains-for-3-each): Intruder’s Tom Steer reports 28 cloud-IP subdomain takeovers, including five Treasury hosts, illustrating how stale DNS records can hand trusted domains to a new address owner.
- [Fingerprinting Jev’s backbone](https://modaic.dev/blog/latency-doesnt-lie): Modaic’s authors combine tokenizer–latency correlations and throughput comparisons to argue that Jev likely derives from a small Qwen mixture-of-experts model; undisclosed hardware and closely related architectures limit the inference.
- [Marginalia’s index experiments](https://www.marginalia.nu/log/a_141_marginalia_update/): Marginalia’s maintainer is cautiously testing a C++ index port for better memory-handling ergonomics and possible speed gains, emphasizing maintainability and independence over a rushed rewrite.
- [The Times’ Photoshop restriction](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1041227/the-new-york-times-warns-photographers-not-to-update-photoshop-over-ai-features): Emma Roth reports that The New York Times barred contributors from using Photoshop 2027 on assignments because unobvious AI features could add misleading provenance metadata.
- [Checking Clojure with Lean and differential tests](https://s.conao3.com/blog/2026/e4f733): The author reports 17 reproduced Clojure 1.12.7 bugs, including nonterminating primitive-vector reduction and a hash-collision lookup failure, while distinguishing proofs of small arithmetic models from tests of larger structures.
- [GitHub’s November 25 outage](https://www.githubstatus.com/incidents/qn8mfv6q5hlx): GitHub attributed widespread service degradation to high database load and reported recovery after changing load distribution; its detailed root-cause analysis was still forthcoming.
- [The evidence behind an “immune reset” headline](https://scienceinhand.com/72-hour-fasting-can-reset-your-entire-immune-system-usc-study-shows/): Lucas Bennett explains that the cited 2014 fasting research mainly involved mice and preliminary chemotherapy observations, establishing no whole-immune-system reset in healthy humans.
- [Macrohistory in six facts](https://basilhalperin.com/essays/macrohistory.html): Basil Halperin contrasts longstanding poverty and industrial acceleration with relatively steady modern frontier growth, highlighting how the historical window changes expectations about future growth.
- [Ubuntu security response times](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/11/25/9): Researcher Hanno Böck reports delays in Ubuntu fixes for serious kernel vulnerabilities and argues that maintaining downstream backports deserves closer scrutiny.
- [Huawei verdict](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/US/2026/11/25/jury-convicts-Huawei/3151795587204/): UPI reports convictions for Huawei and subsidiaries on fraud, trade-secret and Iran-sanctions charges, an acquittal on racketeering conspiracy, and the company’s rejection of the convictions.
- [Qwen across 5,600 Lambdas](https://noahbuilt.it/writing/qwen-on-5600-lambdas): A working distributed inference experiment took 17 seconds to process roughly 4,000 tokens and cost over 80 times the author’s hosted-model comparator, exposing the limits of CPU fan-out for this workload.
- [C++ default constructors](https://lemire.me/blog/2026/11/02/default-and-braces-are-not-the-same-constructor/): Daniel Lemire shows how `= default` and an empty constructor change value initialization, reporting a fourfold performance difference in his GCC 15/16 example that did not appear with GCC 14 or Clang 21.
- [ProofAtlas open problems](https://www.proofatlas.ai/open-problems/): ProofAtlas organizes mathematical questions with statements and status notes, explicitly presenting its importance ranking as model assessment rather than expert consensus or a forecast.
- [Britain’s ghost stations](https://spectator.com/article/britains-ghost-stations-are-fascinating/): Ross Clark distinguishes stations kept nominally open with sparse services from remote stops with regular trains but few passengers, tracing the peculiar legacy of railway closures.
- Gisèle Pelicot plans legal action after intimate photographs were distributed to promote an essay; the publisher apologised and halted further printing and promotion, BBC reports. [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm3eg502nxw2o)
- Northern Irish writers warn that proposed library neutrality rules could enable censorship; the minister defends the policy while declining to guarantee Pride and Black History Month displays. [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/ck1l0d8j7n68o)
- The Society of Authors reports more than 7,000 creators opposing Australia’s proposed AI-training copyright changes, arguing that permission should remain necessary for training on their work. - Marc Winer compares OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s claimed VP–VNP proofs, distinguishing upload timestamps from claimed discovery dates and Lean verification from human checks of the formal statements. [MACCUS](https://maccus.fr/en/openai-vp-vs-vnp-proof/)
- [ActorRPC](https://github.com/elementary-swift/actor-rpc) — Simon Leeb’s early Swift proof of concept shares a protocol between browser and server, turning distributed calls into JSON POST requests; its API and wire format remain unstable.
- [USACiv](https://karpathy.ai/usaciv/) — Andrej Karpathy maps US public data onto a Civilization-style grid; the explanatory notes distinguish metro-wide figures from city boundaries.
- [Wendy Lesser’s Scandinavian Noir](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/books/story/2020-05-01/wendy-lesser-scandinavian-crime-profile) — Paula L. Woods traces Lesser’s reading from Martin Beck to Wallander, then her trip to compare the novels’ social worlds with real Scandinavian policing.
