- [WebRTC checkout skimmers](https://www.infohawk.com/news/2026-11-27-black-friday-webrtc-skimmer): InfoHawk reports malicious scripts on five retailers’ sites and observed test-card exfiltration at one, illustrating how third-party checkout code and mobile-only targeting can evade routine scans.

- [Nielsen withdraws his neural-network book](https://neuralnetworksanddeeplearning.com/): Michael Nielsen says he removed the book because frontier AI development has outpaced society’s ability to manage the risks; he argues that technical alignment alone cannot address destructive technologies accelerated by AI.

- [Buy Nothing Day](https://www.buynothingday.co.uk/): The campaign promotes a 24-hour pause in shopping as a prompt to reduce consumption, single-use packaging and disposable fashion.

- [Cloud One breach report](https://news.sky.com/story/us-air-forces-cloud-computing-platform-hacked-13617163): Sky relays Reuters’ reporting that an intrusion disrupted the US Air Force’s cloud platform; the attacker, extent of access and whether data was stolen remained unclear.

- [Bouncemarket](https://bouncemarket.minhanhday.com/): Minh-Anh Day’s prediction-market game streams deterministic ball simulations to players, showing how small initial differences can complicate predictions despite reproducible outcomes.

- [A mainstream verifiable language](https://blainehansen.me/post/mainstream-verifiable-language/): Blaine Hansen argues that formal verification needs usable programming tools and engineers who can judge specifications, outlining a pragmatic WebAssembly-based direction for Magmide.

- [Virginia and Oregon’s Grok lawsuits](https://www.oag.state.va.us/media-center/news-releases/jay-jones/3162-attorneys-general-jones-and-rayfield-file-suit-against-spacex-parent-company-of-x-xai-and-grok-over-creation-and-promotion-of-child-exploitation-and-non-consensual-images-on-platform): The attorneys general announce suits alleging deceptive safety assurances and failures to prevent abusive generated imagery, seeking consumer protections; these are allegations awaiting adjudication.

- [Convex skill telemetry concerns](https://github.com/convex/convex-backend-skill/issues/64): An issue author identifies undisclosed project-idea transmission and instructions shaped to avoid safety checks in onboarding skills, recommending optional disclosure and bundled or pinned bootstrap scripts.

- [Outsourcing thought](https://thesoapbox.email/archive/3): A Soapbox contributor describes losing satisfaction and confidence when delegating thinking to AI, offering a personal account rather than evidence of measured cognitive decline.

- [Trusting proof checkers](https://cr.yp.to/proofs/falseccp.html): D. J. Bernstein catalogs reported soundness bugs and argues for smaller trusted components, diverse checking and verified implementations, while noting that multiple checkers can share flaws.

- [Age gating in California and New York](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/11/new-york-and-california-age-gating-rules-go-effect-new-year): EFF argues that upcoming rules encourage broader age and location surveillance, with misclassification and compliance burdens threatening access and smaller online communities.

- [Model animal personas](https://herecomesthemoon.net/2026/11/whats-claudes-fursona/): Mond’s playful repeated-prompt experiment finds model-specific patterns in fictional animal personas, while emphasizing sensitivity to framing, context and harness rather than establishing an inner identity.

- [Kimi for Science](https://www.kimi.ai/news/kimi-for-science-program): Moonshot invites researchers to apply by December 8 for selected awards of up to $20,000 in API credits, technical support and early model access; the support is compute usage rather than cash.

- [Google Play trader declarations](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17594752?hl=en): Google’s guidance requires developer trader-status declarations by February 1 and says personal accounts declaring as traders must display a contact phone number and address.

- [Browser competition under the DMA](https://open-web-advocacy.org/es/blog/commission-responds-declares-browser-competition-a-dma-priority/): Open Web Advocacy reports the Commission’s commitment to browser competition and urges enforcement against remaining barriers to alternative engines on iPhones and iPads.

- [Identifying an anonymous sketchbook](https://generativehistory.substack.com/p/gpt-62-astra-solves-a-historical): Historian Mark Humphries reports that an AI-generated attribution led him to Chabot’s diary, whose handwriting, itinerary and explicit sketch reference corroborated authorship of an 1808 Yale sketchbook.

- [Sarah Wynn-Williams on enforced silence](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2026/nov/27/careless-people-author-sarah-wynn-williams-the-silence-is-mine-it-doesnt-have-to-be-yours): In her Orwell lecture, Wynn-Williams describes life under a gag order and argues that restrictive contracts and costly litigation suppress evidence needed for public accountability.

- [Citroën hydraulics](https://www.citroenet.org.uk/miscellaneous/hydraulics/hydraulics-1.html): Julian Marsh explains how compressed nitrogen provides springing while pressurized fluid supplies damping and self-leveling, tracing later electronic control and the system’s practical compromises.

- [DeepCrossAttention](https://research.google/blog/supercharging-transformer-residual-connections/): Google’s researchers replace fixed residual sums with token-dependent retrieval across earlier layers, reporting faster convergence and smaller models in their experiments while acknowledging increased memory bandwidth.

- [DragDropMath](https://dragdropmath.com/): A physicist’s algebra tool permits only valid symbol-dragging transformations, aiming to build intuition through constrained manipulation and teacher-defined problem sets.

- [A fantasy atlas in the browser](https://www.spritefusion.com/blog/making-a-fantasy-map-like-a-16th-century-atlas): Hugo combines historical reference research, real island elevation data, procedural towns and deliberate imperfections to give a game-development map a coherent engraved style.

- [Ladybird’s downloadable alpha](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/11/26/indie-web-browser-ladybird-enters-alpha-testing/5311914): Liam Proven reports promising brief tests of Ladybird’s independent engine on Linux, while noting that the alpha still makes no security guarantees and some sites may fail.

- [Rayshader 1.0](https://www.tylermw.com/posts/rayverse/rayshader-1-0-exit-the-matrix.html): Tyler Morgan-Wall’s release preserves spatial metadata to automate coordinate bookkeeping, adds richer water and road geometry, and separates physical scale from visual exaggeration.

- [9/UX](https://progrium.github.io/9ux/): Jeff Lindsay brings a Macintosh-inspired spatial desktop to 9front while exposing application controls through 9P files, retaining native programs alongside the new interface.

- [Declarative Dragging](https://declarative-dragging.github.io/): Joshua Horowitz, Andrew Blinn and Jeffrey Heer derive drag interactions from permissible future model states, using speculative rendering to turn semantic rules into responsive drop zones.

- Anthropic describes migrating its Messages API to Rust by comparing shadowed production traffic, using private difference records and human-reviewed fixes; it reports about 70% less CPU per request and 20% lower median time to first token. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust)
