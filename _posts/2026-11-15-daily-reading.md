- [PyPI’s security interface](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2026-11-06-ui-updates-complete/) exposes per-file attestations and warns about changes or gaps in release provenance, while stressing that missing attestations alone do not make a package malicious.
- [Lovable application access controls](https://www.crissyfield.de/research/lovable-en/): Crissyfield reports sensitive records exposed by misconfigured Supabase integrations, while warning that its nonrepresentative sample and email-address proxy do not establish a platform-wide leak rate.
- [Bystander privacy](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/11/tech-companies-are-trying-shift-entire-concept-privacy-again): EFF argues that protections for wearable owners leave people around them without meaningful control over ambient recording, transcripts and summaries.
- [Monkey Island’s many editions](https://monkeyisland.pages.dev/) compares original scenes across platform and display variants, with notes on palettes, interfaces, music and storage formats.
- [ggml 1.0](https://github.com/ggml-org/ggml/releases/tag/v1.0.0) removes deprecated interfaces and adds backend-specific performance work, including mixture-of-experts routing, attention kernels and avoiding transfers of unused inputs.
- [Italy’s Meta privacy fine](https://www.reuters.com/world/italy-fines-meta-887-million-over-android-users-data-access-2026-11-14/): Reuters reports an €805 million penalty over gallery access and a demand for separate consent; Meta disputes the decision and plans to appeal.
- [Software job titles](https://zwischenzugs.com/2026/11/03/a-family-tree-of-software-job-titles/): Ian Miell traces recurring splits, mergers and reused names across engineering roles, presenting the family tree’s dates as approximate interpretations rather than sourced first occurrences.
- [Nvidia’s financing arrangements](https://www.techspot.com/news/114558-nvidia-deliberately-kept-gpu-prices-high-through-circular.html): Daniel Sims relays reporting that customer financing absorbed excess chip supply, while attributing the claim that it helped maintain scarcity and prices rather than establishing that conclusion independently.
- [Elephant social networks](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/elephants-found-to-be-increasingly-isolated-leading-to-fractured-social-networks): researchers following more than 440 females in Sri Lanka report smaller, less-connected communities after accumulated population losses, suggesting social change can accelerate beyond a threshold.
- [DeepSeek V3 in JAX](https://github.com/Vaibhaav-Tiwari/deepseek-v3-jax) implements core architectural ideas in a small educational model, documenting routing and attention alongside simplifications that keep it far from the original model’s scale and capabilities.
- [Project Zero’s disclosure timetable](https://projectzero.google/2026/11/disclosure-policy.html): Natalie Silvanovich announces fixed disclosure at 90 days, moving to 60 in February 2027 and 30 in January 2028, citing faster discovery and exploitation and a need to reduce patch delays.
- [Google’s research agent teams](https://research.google/blog/advancing-science-engineering-with-antigravitys-teamwork-feature/): Vahab Mirrokni describes separating candidate generation from independent checks and fresh-context review, reporting applications across formal proofs, molecular analysis and systems optimization.
- [Direct File’s archived source](https://github.com/IRS-Public/direct-file) preserves interview-based tax logic and a reusable fact graph for incomplete information; its maintainers identify the code as historical, unmaintained and unsuitable for production.
- [The Claude Delusion](https://www.netmeister.org/blog/claude-delusion.html): Jan Schaumann argues that coding success and wealth can foster misplaced confidence about unrelated fields, and that describing software as autonomous can blur its operators’ responsibility.
- [Ad-based surveillance](https://citizenlab.ca/security-agencies-worldwide-found-to-use-ad-based-surveillance-system/): Citizen Lab summarizes reporting identifying more government customers of Webloc, extending its earlier investigation into tracking built on advertising data.
- [Terminactory](https://gitlab.com/snipedbywifi/terminactory) is a terminal factory game with deterministic production, offline progress and optional messages from coding agents, keeping its simulation separate from the interface.
- [Formalizing tax rules](https://lexifina.com/blog/formalizing-the-us-tax-code): Alan Yahya describes Lean checks and comparisons with independent calculators, while acknowledging that translating statutes is lossy and the findings have not been reviewed by a lawyer or CPA.
- [Serving one model on two stations](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/when-one-gpu-isnt-enough-serving-glm-5-3-across-two-nvidia-dgx-stations-with-vllm/): NVIDIA reports peak aggregate throughput at 16 concurrent requests, with performance falling at 32, showing why throughput and each user’s latency need separate measurement.
- [Watching reinforcement learning](https://aatishb.com/myriad/how-brains-learn/): Aatish Bhatia and Arkarup Banerjee use a tiny simulated creature to visualize how random parameter changes and reward feedback produce different behaviors, while explaining the model’s simplifying assumptions.
- [Agweb](https://github.com/paul-nameless/agweb) presents a visible WebKit browser for multiple coding agents, with separate workspaces, change-focused responses and idle-tab suspension intended to reduce disruption and resource use.
- [Dartmouth’s mathematics admissions pause](https://www.thedartmouth.com/article/2026/11/gandhi-fagell-guarini-will-not-admit-math-ph-d-cohort-this-year): Alex Fagell and Sahil Gandhi report no incoming 2027–28 PhD cohort during a department review, while existing students retain their funding guarantees and degree support.
- [Phone forensic tools](https://osservatorionessuno.org/blog/2026/05/demystifying-phone-unlocking-tools-a-technical-overview/): Osservatorio Nessuno explains why device state, hardware-backed keys and exposed peripheral drivers shape extraction risks, distinguishing a locked screen from data protected before first unlock.
- Lyra Rebane traces how her joke about an edited bug-tracker reward field became a false million-dollar bounty story in AI-generated news and search summaries, even after she published a correction. [Lyra’s account](https://lyra.horse/blog/2026/11/disinformation-machine/)
- CNN’s FAA coverage examines the disruption after Friday’s ground stop and the competing explanations for the failure, including officials’ accounts of overloaded older systems and controllers’ doubts about the timing of an upgrade. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/13/us/live-news/flights-ground-stop-faa)
- [Louis Anslow](https://newsletter.pessimistsarchive.org/p/how-isaac-asimov-predicted-the-openai) reads Asimov’s “Trends” as a story about resistance to technological change and draws a pointed analogy with the 2023 OpenAI board crisis.
