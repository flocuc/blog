- [Huang’s reinstatement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director): Nvidia’s board says it unanimously restored Jensen Huang after government input, while continuing its independent investigation and adding compliance oversight.
- [Congressional scrutiny of Nvidia](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/nvidia-jensen-huang-congress-trump.html): Kevin Breuninger and Justin Papp report bipartisan demands for answers about export controls and the administration’s role in Huang’s return, with congressional appearances still scheduled.
- [Ladybird’s alpha](https://ladybird.org/posts/alpha/): Andreas Kling announces macOS and Linux builds of the independent browser engine, with rolling updates and automated testing support, while warning about crashes, compatibility gaps and unfinished security.
- [LeCun on open models](https://twitter.com/ylecun/status/2124550037912920090): Yann LeCun argues that Amodei’s warnings favor restrictions that would preserve control for closed-model developers; his claim about Amodei’s motives is an opinion.
- [GLM-5.4’s loop architecture](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.4): Z.ai describes reusing 28 layers for two passes with gated updates and shared sparse attention state, reporting reasoning gains while acknowledging prolonged thinking loops; model weights remain a future release plan.
- [Jon’s Arm Reference](https://arm.jonpalmisc.com/): Jon says Arm’s lawyers requested the documentation site’s removal and announces a December 4 closure, while considering ways to preserve existing links.
- [XNU Linux](https://github.com/AFK-surf/xnulinux) reports running macOS userspace through a hosted XNU kernel module alongside Linux, with graphics and memory limitations and relaxed platform protections that make it an experimental compatibility project.
- [FSNotes’ EU removal](https://github.com/glushchenko/fsnotes/discussions/2056): The developer says Apple removed the app from European stores and left support requests unanswered despite submitted compliance documents; free source and builds remain available.
- [Kagi Mail’s public beta](https://blog.kagi.com/kagi-mail-public-beta) combines hosted email and calendars with standard IMAP, JMAP and CalDAV access, optional AI features and export support; contacts and native mobile apps remain planned additions.
- [Nospeaker](https://github.com/os6/nospeaker): Cody Collier describes synthesizing speech through a MacBook trackpad’s haptic motor, reducing voice audio to two changing resonances; the experiment relies on private macOS interfaces and has limited hardware testing.
- [Seventeen million lines of Lean](https://micjm.com/blog/yes-17m-lines-of-lean-is-a-lot/): micjm compares repository sizes and distinguishes proof code from generated database checks, showing why raw line counts need context when comparing formalization projects.
- [Anthropic’s VP–VNP artifact](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/vp-vnp) presents an AI-written algebraic-complexity proof and Lean formalization, explicitly stating that no human mathematician has checked the proof and that the result would not settle P versus NP.
- [The next circle of security hell](https://blogs.gentoo.org/mgorny/2026/10/27/the-next-circle-of-security-hell/): Michał Górny describes how report floods, context-blind scanners and security fixes bundled with breaking changes increase maintainers’ workload and can undermine downstream reliability.
- [Stalwart’s Exchange compatibility](https://stalw.art/blog/microsoft-exchange-replacement/): Mauro D. announces MAPI, EWS, ActiveSync and Autodiscover support as a bridge for existing clients, while documenting missing Exchange features and excluding the cloud-dependent new Outlook.
- [Win4wine](https://github.com/mikuta0407/win4wine) experimentally runs the Windows XP shell over patched Wine as a Linux desktop, integrating native Linux windows while requiring users’ own Windows media; its author warns it is unfinished for everyday use.
- [Mapping the Riemann formalization](https://pengowray.github.io/riemann-zeta-proof-visualizer/): A treemap separates module size, repeated statements and dependencies, while warning that mathematical-field labels are statistical estimates and that reported verification checks were run by others.
- [Moving SSH keys into an agent](https://jola.dev/posts/you-cant-afford-ssh-keys-disk): Johanna Larsson describes replacing loose private-key files with Bitwarden’s SSH agent and a separate commit-signing key, balancing approval prompts with her chosen vault-lock interval.
- [NariQ’s low-bit Qwen release](https://huggingface.co/nari-labs/Qwen3.8-27B-NariQ-GGUF): Nari Labs reports near-BF16 results for its 3-bit and 2.5-bit files on selected agent and reasoning tests, with weaker 2-bit results; the format currently needs its own runtime fork and has substantial hardware limits.
- [Docker’s new positioning](https://www.docker.com/blog/meet-the-new-docker-built-to-run-on-trust/): CMO Terri Avnaim frames Docker’s rebrand around agent isolation, governance and supply-chain trust, describing a broader strategy rather than a new container replacement.
- [Tempo’s partial evaluator](https://phoenix.labri.fr/software/tempo/) separates analysis of known and unknown inputs from specialization, using alias and side-effect analysis to produce C programs tailored to fixed inputs at compile time or runtime.
- [Alpöge on the write-up](https://twitter.com/__alpoge__/status/2124987173439209561): Levent Alpöge says the quarter-million-page remark was a joke, accepts criticism of the Lean code’s clutter, and expects a substantially simpler mathematical presentation to emerge.
- [A wireless SNES adapter](https://boland.dev/snet/): Danny Boland combines a controller-port serial link with Wi-Fi for WebDAV save synchronization, preserving conflicts instead of overwriting them, and experiments with cached dialogue to hide language-model latency.
- [Amodei’s reported games proposal](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1039745/dario-amodei-video-games-ai-joblessness): Terrence O’Brien relays a Wall Street Journal account of old Slack discussions in which Amodei reportedly proposed immersive games as a response to political unrest from AI job losses.
- Apple's Depth Pro 2 estimates metric depth from photos and video, using cross-frame attention to improve temporal consistency; its released weights are restricted to non-commercial research. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/apple/DepthPro2)
- Eric Berger reports rising visits and registrations at several US public libraries, where patrons seek books, social connection, equipment loans and affordable activities. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2026/nov/17/public-libraries-visitor-surge)
- A Guardian editorial argues that *The Selfish Gene* remains an influential model of engaging science writing while urging readers to consider its scientific critics. [Editorial](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/oct/30/the-guardian-view-on-50-years-of-the-selfish-gene-flawed-outdated-yet-still-a-model-of-truly-popular-science-writing)
- CrowdStrike announces an agreement to acquire Zscaler for approximately $43.9 billion in stock, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals. [Announcement](https://ir.crowdstrike.com/news-releases/news-release-details/crowdstrike-acquire-zscaler-bringing-network-security-and-zero/)
- [Unison 2.0 release notes](https://github.com/unisonweb/unison/releases/tag/release%2F2.0.2) describe an optional LLVM JIT, anonymous records, experimental standalone executables and MCP tools separated by the effects they permit.
