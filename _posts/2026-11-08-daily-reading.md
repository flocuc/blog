- [A $10 OpenStick homelab](https://blog.bosscher.ch/posts/openstick/): Laurens Bosscher runs Debian, DNS blocking, RSS, bookmarks and monitoring on a silent 512 MB device, choosing small services over a heavier monitoring stack.
- [Underground 2 Revived](https://nfsu2.xyz/) recompiles the PC racing game for Chrome and Firefox, with online races and free roaming; its creator began the port to play again with his brother.
- [Apple’s personal knowledge graph](https://8rank.ai/game/apple-intelligence-personal-knowledge-graph): Dario Pasquini and Michał Bazyli trace local relationship inference from contacts, photos and communication metadata, and release a read-only viewer; the database is protected by Full Disk Access.
- [The end of AI Frontiers](https://hunch.net/?p=13764006): John Langford says he left Microsoft after its reorganisation left his research without a path to use, arguing that model scaling and fundamental research should develop together.
- [Apple’s predictive-text grey list](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/06/struggling-to-spell-male-chauvinist-pig-apple-grey-list-means-your-iphone-wont-help-you): Aisha Down reports research finding over 100,000 restricted autocomplete phrases; Apple says users can still type them explicitly.
- [Asahi Linux’s progress report](https://asahilinux.org/2026/11/progress-report-7-3/): James Calligeros announces general M3 support and limited Thunderbolt support; experimental per-process page sizes improve Windows-game performance, but are not yet shipping.
- [Running a law firm on Free software](https://decoded.legal/blog/2026/11/running-a-law-firm-on-free-software-2026-edition/): Neil Brown describes five years of Debian and self-hosting, replacing little-used services with simpler tools while retaining compatibility with clients’ documents and meetings.
- [Discord’s Rust client](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/11/06/every-windows-11-app-should-kill-electron-and-discord-just-showed-why-no-starting-screen-and-70-less-ram-even-with-two-accounts-signed-in/): Abhijith M B reports faster cold starts and a 290 MB memory snapshot with two accounts; Discord has not released a like-for-like benchmark or public launch date.
- [MaxxBench](https://maxxbench.unlap.ai/research/why-maxxbench/): Gavin and Justin Lo find that small changes to 89 coding tasks separate agents with similar original scores; some agents explicitly favour imagined hidden tests over revised requirements.
- [The $8 Linux computer](http://thelittleengineerthatcould.blogspot.com/2022/12/the-8-linux-computer.html) documents the Ox64’s early flashing hurdles; this 2022 instalment reaches a working serial connection, with networking and storage still unavailable.
- [Secrets in coding-agent files](https://apiiro.com/blog/coding-agents-leaking-secrets-github-dont-ask-again): Apiiro reports 2,539 credential-like secrets across public repositories and history, tracing many to literal commands saved in approval rules; the researchers did not test credential validity.
- [Coldframe](https://github.com/hedgerow-dev/coldframe) imitates supermarket refrigeration controllers to record attempted logins, exploits and follow-up actions without controlling real equipment; its creator initially saw only generic scanners.
- [Turbo Pascal’s economy](https://kevinboone.me/tpwin.html): Kevin Boone recalls how a compiler built for tight memory and slow disks made integrated Windows development unusually fast and approachable.
- [The components of agent memory](https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory): Anthropic finds that offline checks against transcripts can repair false memories, while linked notes help most when tasks require combining documents rather than finding isolated facts.
- [How long can life wait?](https://mceglowski.substack.com/p/how-long-can-life-wait): Maciej Cegłowski separates evidence of microbial survival over roughly 100,000 years from disputed claims spanning millions, showing why contamination controls matter for Mars exploration.
- [Terry Pratchett’s study in miniature](https://www.sussi.dk/terry-pratchetts-study): Sussi describes a 1:12 tribute built over six weeks, with individual book covers, a hand-sewn leather jacket and a correctly ordered miniature manuscript.
- [Five memorable programming books](https://prog21.dadgum.com/19.html): James Hague values books that show serious work in a language and explain how to decompose problems, rather than teaching syntax through contrived exercises.
- [Putting the AI sell-off in perspective](https://www.kadoa.com/companies/insights/ai-selloff-perspective): Kadoa’s November 6 price-index comparison shows a much steeper drawdown in semiconductor stocks than the S&P 500, while historical recovery times exclude dividends.
- [A processor-die atlas](https://sheets.works/data-viz/cpu-dies) gathers 356 freely licensed photographs spanning 1972–2024, with a common-scale view for chips whose dimensions are known and credit retained for each photographer.
- [Making open messaging useful](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50405250): Michael Lynch describes the gap between his preference for open protocols and his family’s priorities of simple, reliable messaging, finding XMPP and Matrix clients difficult to use.
- [GitHub’s new pull-request overview](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-11-05-new-pull-request-overview-page-in-public-preview/) puts merge blockers and next actions at the top, with filters for unresolved conversations and a newest-first comment view in an optional public preview.
- [Making any integer with four 2s](https://eli.thegreenplace.net/2025/making-any-integer-with-four-2s/): Eli Bendersky explains Dirac’s construction using repeated square roots and logarithms, turning a puzzle about clever expressions into a general solution.
- [HyperFlow H3 Flash](https://github.com/Video-Rebirth/hyperflow-h3-flash): Video Rebirth compresses MiniMax-H3’s roughly 31-billion-parameter transformer to under 14 billion and uses four-to-eight sampling steps for synchronized video and audio; its measurements distinguish consumer-GPU and data-centre runtimes.
- [Port zero on the wire](https://acid.vegas/blog/the-port-that-does-not-exist/): Acidvegas distinguishes the sockets API’s special meaning of zero from TCP traffic, reporting exposed services that ordinary clients and default scans often miss.
- [GeoWorks’ lost opportunity](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/the-history-of-geoworks-microsoft-windows-upstart-90s-competitor): Ernie Smith traces a lightweight desktop system’s limited developer ecosystem and later life in PDAs, Nokia communicators and electronic typewriters.
- [Retro Song Bird](https://www.retrosongbird.com/) presents a player that takes Game Boy and Game Boy Color cartridges to play their soundtracks.
- [The Gordon Gekko effect](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/2016/epr_2016_gordon-gekko-effect_lo.pdf): Andrew W. Lo argues that financial risk management must account for leadership, peer norms and institutional feedback, proposing independent accident investigations to help firms learn from failures.
- Roy Medina’s Observer watches screen, camera or audio inputs for user-defined events, running models locally or through a configurable server before triggering notifications. [Project](https://github.com/Roy3838/Observer)
- Joel Niklaus’s Harness Leaderboard compares coding agents with models, providers and reasoning effort held constant; several leading harnesses remain statistically tied, and repeated runs expose substantial task-level noise. [Benchmark](https://huggingface.co/spaces/joelniklaus/harness-leaderboard)
- Michael Larabel reports two Vulkan 1.4.368 extensions: Valve’s vertex-address binding optimization and NVIDIA’s access to opaque internal driver handles for use with external APIs. [Release report](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Vulkan-1.4.368)
- A merged llama.cpp change runs selected MoE cache misses on the CPU and adds hardware-specific tuning; its example shows that increasing CPU work can help a small cache but hurt a larger one. [Pull request](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/30966)
- Sahil Sen and colleagues find that grep beats vector retrieval with inline results on their 116-question conversation-memory sample, but the ranking can reverse when results move through files; they caution against generalizing beyond the tested setting. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15184)
- Immich 3 adds configurable nightly integrity reports that identify untracked files, missing files and checksum mismatches, alongside non-destructive mobile editing and workflow previews. [Release announcement](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/discussions/29439)
- Anil Madhavapeddy surveys OxCaml 5.4.0+ox9, including unboxed layouts, explicit atomic-field updates, safer effects and compiler warnings for misleading kind annotations. [OxCaml release notes](https://anil.recoil.org/notes/oxcaml-ox9-improvements).
